How to calculate epoxy for a flood coat

Rectangle: Volume = Length × Width × Thickness
Round: Volume = π × (Diameter ÷ 2)² × Thickness
US fl oz = in³ ÷ 1.805 · ml = cm³

Rule of thumb

At ⅛ inch, one US gallon covers about 12 square feet (1.2 m²). If your surface is porous wood, brush on a thin seal coat first. It soaks in, and it stops bubbles from rising into the flood coat.

Frequently asked questions

How much epoxy do I need to coat a table?

Multiply length × width × coat thickness. For a standard ⅛ inch flood coat, one US gallon covers about 12 square feet. Add around 15% for drips off the edges.

How thick is a flood coat of epoxy?

Most tabletop epoxies self-level at about ⅛ inch (3 mm) per coat. Thicker coats can overheat or run off, so build up thickness with multiple coats.

How much resin do I need for a resin art canvas?

A thin artistic layer is about 1/16 inch (0.0625). A 12 × 12 inch canvas needs roughly 5 fl oz (150 ml) at that thickness, plus 15–20% for the edges.
