Volume vs. Weight: The #1 Epoxy Failure Reason

A huge proportion of cured resin failures (sticky spots, tacky top layers, cloudy finishes) happen because makers measure by weight on a kitchen scale using volume proportions.

Part A (epoxy resin) is considerably denser and heavier than Part B (polyamine or amine hardener). A typical 2:1 volume system weighs out to approximately 100 parts A to 45 parts B (or 2.22:1 by weight). If you weigh 2:1 on a scale, you starve the mixture of hardener!

Volume Basis: A_vol / B_vol = Ratio_A / Ratio_B
Weight Basis: A_wt / B_wt = Ratio_A_wt / Ratio_B_wt
Total Batch = Part A + Part B

Quick Mixing Protocol

  1. Pour Part A into a clean, graduated container.
  2. Add exact calculated amount of Part B.
  3. Mix thoroughly for at least 3 minutes, scraping sides and bottom continuously.
  4. Pour into a second clean container ("double pot mixing") and mix for another 1 minute to avoid unmixed residue from walls.

Frequently asked questions

Can I measure 2:1 epoxy by weight?

Not using 2:1! Epoxies rated 2:1 by volume typically require around 100:43 to 100:45 by weight because resin (Part A) is denser than hardener (Part B). Always verify the weight ratio on your manufacturer data sheet.

What happens if I add extra hardener to cure faster?

Never add extra hardener. Unlike polyester fiberglass resin where catalyst amount controls cure speed, epoxy cures via stoichiometric chemical cross-linking. Excess hardener leaves unreacted molecules, resulting in permanently sticky, rubbery, or yellowed resin.

How do I calculate hardener if I have a fixed amount of resin left?

Select 'From Part A' in the calculator above. If your ratio is 2:1 and you have 200g of Part A, you need 200 / 2 = 100g of Part B for a total of 300g.
